## The Problem with Current Cosmology

Modern cosmology has three enormous unsolved problems. Each one represents something we can observe, measure, and confirm — but cannot explain. Each one has been given a name that sounds like an explanation but is actually just a label for a mystery.

**Dark Matter** is the name we give to gravitational effects that cannot be accounted for by visible matter. Galaxies rotate at speeds that should tear them apart given the matter we can see. Gravitational lensing — the bending of light by gravity — occurs in regions where there is insufficient visible matter to cause it. Something is providing the gravitational effect. We have never detected it directly despite decades of increasingly sensitive experiments. We call it dark matter and move on.

**Dark Energy** is the name we give to the accelerating expansion of the universe. The universe is not just expanding — it is expanding faster over time. Something is driving that acceleration. We do not know what it is. We inserted a term into Einstein's field equations — the cosmological constant — to make the math fit the observations. That term has no known physical mechanism. We call it dark energy and move on.

**Inflation** is the name we give to the hypothesized period of near-instantaneous exponential expansion immediately after the Big Bang. Without it, the universe should not look as uniform as it does at large scales. The proposed mechanism is an inflaton field driven by an inflaton particle. Neither has ever been detected. The inflaton was invented to make the model work. We call it inflation and move on.

Three separate unexplained mechanisms. Three invented fields or particles with no observational confirmation. Three labels on three mysteries. The hypothesis presented here replaces all three with one process, uses no new physics, requires no new particles, and follows entirely from a single assumption about what actually happens at a singularity.

## The Single Assumption

Current physics assumes that matter falling into a black hole is preserved. It may be scrambled, encoded, transformed — but the information and mass-energy are conserved somewhere within or upon the black hole system. This assumption is called unitarity. It is a deeply held theoretical prior. It has never been experimentally confirmed at singularity conditions.

The Rip hypothesis makes a different assumption:

> *Matter under sufficient gravitational stress crosses a geometric threshold into a child geometry, exiting this universe entirely.*

From the perspective of our universe, matter is gone. From the perspective of the child geometry, matter has arrived. Nothing is destroyed. Nothing is lost. The books balance — just not within a single universe. Information crosses intact. Conservation laws hold everywhere, across the whole nested structure.

This is the Rip. The tearing of the boundary between nested geometries under sufficient gravitational stress, through which matter transits from one universe to another.

## Two Regimes of the Same Process

The Rip does not operate as a binary. It has two regimes determined by the intensity of gravitational stress at the threshold:

### Full Rip

At sufficient mass and density, matter crosses the threshold completely. From our universe's perspective it is gone. The geometry it created — the gravitational curvature — persists temporarily and then disperses, contributing to the expansion of our universe. The energy that drove the expansion came from the mass-energy of the matter that crossed. This is what we observe as the accelerating expansion of the universe and attribute to dark energy.

### Partial Rip

Below the full threshold, matter is partially processed. Some crosses. Some does not. The geometry created by the matter that crossed persists as a stable gravitational well — there is no matter at its center but the curvature remains. This persistent geometry has mass-equivalent gravitational effects. It does not interact electromagnetically because there is nothing there to interact. It is invisible to every instrument designed to detect particles. This is what we observe as dark matter.

One process. Two regimes. The threshold condition between them is determined by local mass, density, and gravitational field intensity — all calculable from existing physics without new parameters.

## What Follows From This

With a single assumption and two regimes, the following observations are explained without additional mechanisms:

### Inflation

The early universe was extraordinarily dense. At sufficient density, matter does not form stars before collapsing — it goes directly to black hole conditions. Full Rip events at enormous scale in the early universe would have produced exactly the near-instantaneous exponential expansion that inflation describes, without requiring an inflaton field or particle. The expansion tapered as the initial density dropped below the full Rip threshold and conditions stabilized.

### Accelerating Expansion

Full Rip events continue throughout cosmic history wherever black holes form and consume matter. Each event transfers mass-energy out of our universe, reducing the gravitational braking on expansion. As more stars die and more black holes form over time, the rate of mass-energy removal increases, and the expansion accelerates. No dark energy field is required. The acceleration is the cumulative bookkeeping effect of ongoing Rip events.

### Dark Matter Halos

The persistent gravitational wells left by partial Rip events distribute through space wherever sub-threshold conditions occurred in the early universe. These wells attract normal matter, which flows along gravitational gradients toward concentration points. Galaxies form at those points. The surrounding network of wells remains as the dark matter halo — not a product of the galaxy but its scaffold. The halo came first. The galaxy formed around it.

### Cosmic Web Structure

The large-scale structure of the universe — matter distributed in filaments and sheets surrounding vast empty voids — reflects the distribution of early universe Rip events. Voids are regions where full Rip events consumed matter completely, leaving only dispersed geometry. Filaments are regions of partial Rip well networks along which normal matter later flowed. The cosmic web is the fossil record of early universe Rip activity.

### Stars as Threshold Transients

Between ordinary matter and a black hole lies a band of gravitational stress deep enough to ignite fusion but not deep enough to cross the Rip threshold. Matter concentrating toward a Full Rip passes through this band on its way down. A star is matter caught in that band — a gravity well on its way to becoming a black hole but not yet arrived. This makes stars a transient population rather than a stable endpoint: an ignited concentration either continues to accrete and crosses the threshold into a black hole, or burns its matter away and falls back below the ignition band and goes dark. Because the early universe drove enormous quantities of matter down this gradient quickly, a burst of star formation coincident with the early black hole formation epoch follows directly — matching the observed early rapid star formation seen alongside early black holes, without requiring separate mechanisms for the two.

### Anomalous Gravitational Lensing

Gravitational lensing detected in regions with insufficient visible matter is explained directly. The lensing is caused by persistent partial Rip wells — stable geometry without matter at the center. There is no invisible particle to detect because there is no particle. The curvature is real. The matter that created it is in a child universe.

### Ultra-Massive Black Holes

Supermassive black holes in the early universe are too large too soon for standard accretion models. Under the Rip hypothesis, black holes grow not only by accreting local matter but by receiving matter from the parent universe crossing the threshold inward. A black hole growing from both directions simultaneously would reach observed masses on timescales that standard single-direction accretion cannot explain. The most massive black holes mark locations of highest parent universe matter flux.

### Free Quarks

Partial Rip events operating at the atomic scale may strip quarks from nuclei mid-process — some crossing the threshold, the remainder stranded in our universe before quark confinement mechanisms can respond. Free quarks produced this way would have fractional electric charges, no electromagnetic interaction pathway, and effectively zero likelihood of detection by instruments designed around integer charges and bound states. They would be distributed diffusely through intergalactic space as a component of the dark matter background.

## The Universe We Are In

The Rip process has no minimum scale requirement. It operates wherever sufficient gravitational stress exists. Applied consistently, this means our universe — which began as a singularity, expanded rapidly, and shows all the signatures of Rip activity — is itself the interior of a black hole in a parent universe.

The Big Bang was not matter appearing from nothing. It was matter from the parent universe crossing the Rip threshold and becoming the substrate of our universe. The inflation period was the Rip event itself. The subsequent structure emerged from the geometry seeded by that crossing.

This produces an infinite nested structure — universes within black holes within universes — each one appearing from the inside to have begun at a singularity, each one unaware of the others, information conserved across the whole hierarchy. It closely parallels Lee Smolin's cosmological natural selection proposal, arrived at through a different mechanism.

## The One-Way Membrane

The event horizon is a one-way boundary by definition. Light and matter from the parent universe can cross inward into our universe. Nothing from our universe can cross outward. This creates a specific and permanent asymmetry:

- The parent universe can see into ours — all our light, all our history, all our structure is visible to them as the interior of one of their black holes
- We cannot see out — our cosmological horizon is the inside surface of our event horizon
- Parent universe light crossing into ours arrives blueshifted by the horizon crossing, with no interior travel history and no identifiable source
- Our existence leaks outward only as Hawking radiation — thermal noise, not a message

If the parent universe black hole that contains us is still accreting — still feeding — then matter and light from the parent universe are still crossing the threshold into ours continuously. This ongoing input would appear as new matter arriving at our cosmological boundary with no prior history in our universe.

## Testable Predictions

A hypothesis that cannot be tested is not useful. The Rip hypothesis makes specific predictions that are in principle checkable against existing or near-future observational data:

- **CMB boundary anomalies should cluster geometrically** rather than distributing randomly. The cold spot, hemispherical asymmetry, and large-scale alignment anomalies should show geometric relationships consistent with boundary conditions rather than statistical fluctuation.

- **Ultra-high energy cosmic rays** exceeding the GZK limit should arrive preferentially from boundary directions and carry energy profiles inconsistent with interior travel. Two non-exclusive Rip mechanisms could produce them: parent universe photons arriving blueshifted by horizon crossing; and parent-side infall matter that approached but did not cross a Rip threshold, retaining the kinetic energy of that infall and emerging into our universe at extreme velocity with no local accelerator to account for it. The second mechanism predicts massive particles, not only photons, originating near Rip boundaries — a spectral and compositional signature distinguishable from interior-accelerated cosmic rays.

- **Matter density at the cosmological horizon** should be slightly elevated above what interior models predict, reflecting ongoing matter arrival from the parent universe.

- **Expansion rate variations** should not correlate perfectly with internal black hole populations. Episodic deviations would reflect feeding cycle variations in the parent universe's accretion onto our black hole.

- **Element depletion ratios near galactic halos** should show hydrogen depleted at higher rates than heavier elements in regions of high Rip activity, reflecting differential vulnerability of simpler nuclei to partial threshold crossing.

- **Dark matter halo density should increase over time** rather than remaining static, reflecting the feedback loop between well accumulation and matter capture.

Several of these predictions are checkable against existing datasets. The CMB anomaly data exists. The cosmic ray arrival direction and energy data exists. Reanalysis without the isotropy assumption — looking for boundary structure rather than filtering it as noise — is a computational task, not an observational one.
